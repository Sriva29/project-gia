Data Flow

The contract is a handoff agreement. It keeps data loading, MCP tools, deterministic policy logic, and final output aligned.

1. Source Facts

One current inventory snapshot and 28 daily sales rows for each store and SKU.

>

2. Deterministic Calculator

Computes velocity, days of supply, reorder point, reorder quantity, and clearance eligibility.

>

3. Structured Output

Writes recommendation records with evidence, reasoning, confidence factors, and run metadata.

Records in Plain English

Each record answers one question. If a field does not help answer that question, it probably belongs somewhere else.

Input

InventorySnapshot

What is physically and operationally true for this SKU in this store right now?

  • Store, SKU, product name, category
  • On hand, reserved, and on order units
  • Age, lead time, minimum stock, case pack
Input

SalesDaily

How many units sold on a specific business date?

  • One row per store, SKU, and date
  • Zero-sales days must still be present
  • Missing days mean incomplete evidence
Output

Recommendation

What should the business review, and what evidence supports it?

  • Type: slow mover, reorder, or clearance
  • Decision, action, evidence, and reasoning
  • Confidence score with explainable factors
Output

RunMetadata

What happened during the whole run?

  • Store, as-of date, status, and policy version
  • Input and output counts
  • Freshness, model usage, warnings, and errors

Three Policy Decisions

These are intentionally simple for Phase 1. The goal is trustworthy behavior before smarter behavior.

Slow Mover

Flags stock that is moving slowly enough to deserve attention.

  • 28-day units sold are at or below 4, or days of supply is at least 60.
  • Sellable stock must be at least 5 units.
  • Sales history must be complete.

Reorder

Flags stock that is at or below the reorder point.

  • Default lead time is 7 days.
  • Safety stock is 3 days of demand, with a 2-unit floor when demand exists.
  • Reorder quantity rounds up to the case pack.

Clearance

Flags older excess stock for markdown review.

  • Inventory age must be at least 45 days.
  • Days of supply must be at least 45, or demand must be zero.
  • Markdown bands are 10%, 20%, and 30%.

Core Formulas

The formulas are the real decision engine. The agent explains them, but it does not get to replace them.

average_daily_sales = units_sold_lookback / 28
sellable_units = max(on_hand_units - reserved_units, 0)
inventory_position_units = sellable_units + on_order_units
days_of_supply = sellable_units / average_daily_sales
reorder_point = max(minimum_stock, ceil(avg_sales * lead_time + safety_stock))

Why this matters

  • 1The output can be tested against known fixture cases.
  • 2Business reviewers can trace every recommendation back to evidence.
  • 3The LLM adds clarity, not hidden business logic.

What Gets Written

The MVP writes machine-readable files first. CSV is an export view, not the source of truth.

File Purpose Review focus
recommendations.json Full run envelope with metadata and recommendation records. Can every recommendation be validated against schema and evidence?
recommendations.csv Spreadsheet-friendly export for business review. Are nested evidence fields readable enough, or do we need fixed CSV columns?
run-metadata.json Status, counts, freshness, warnings, errors, and model details. Can we tell whether a run succeeded and whether data was fresh?

Feedback Prompts

These are the decisions most worth reviewing before implementation starts.

Are the thresholds believable?

Review 4 units sold, 60 days of supply, 45 inventory-age days, and markdown bands of 10%, 20%, and 30%.

Are the records understandable?

Check whether InventorySnapshot, SalesDaily, Recommendation, and RunMetadata feel like the right boundaries.

Is CSV output specific enough?

The contract currently allows nested evidence as compact JSON strings. That is easy to implement, but less friendly in spreadsheets.

What should count as insufficient data?

The current contract is strict: missing days are not silently treated as zero sales.
